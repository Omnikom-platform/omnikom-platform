# Document title goes here
One-line subtitle: what this document is and who it is for

The lede: two or three plain sentences on the cover saying what the reader gets from this document and what you need from them. It sits on the dark cover next to a lime bar.

## A section with bullet points
- **Lead phrase in bold:** then the plain sentence that explains it. Keep each bullet to one idea.
- **Another point:** bullets get a small square marker and a thin rule between them.
- **You get:** a bullet that starts with "You get:" turns into a highlighted outcome box, for the result of a step.

## A section with a plain paragraph

Plain paragraphs need a blank line before them. Use them for context that is not a list: one short paragraph, plain words, no jargon.
- **And then bullets again:** paragraphs and bullets can mix in one section.

## A section with questions to fill in
1. **Must answer.** A question the reader has to answer. Fill-in lines look like this: ______
   Why we ask: one line saying why the answer matters.
2. A question with tick boxes: ☐ First option · ☐ Second option · ☐ Not sure
   Why it matters: the label of this line can be "Why we ask", "Why it matters" or "When".
3. A question with a tick table, one box per row and column.
   Columns: Mine | Theirs | Not sure
   - First thing to tick
   - Second thing to tick
