# Other US states' comprehensive privacy laws (checked on 2026-11-21)

Load this file for D3 in `decide.md`, only for the states in `business.states_served` other than California. Each entry
gives the test as a decision on the facts. "Records" means the personal data of the state's residents the business
controls or processes in a year, from every source, leaving out data used only to complete a payment (where the law says
so). Unless an entry says otherwise, a consumer request is answered within 45 days, with one 45-day extension.

Statuses: CONFIRMED = read on the legislature's own page on 2026-11-21. ARCHIVE = the official text was read, but through
a web.archive.org copy of the official page because the official host refused this machine (counted as UNCONFIRMED until
someone reads the live page). UNCONFIRMED = not read on any official text.

Which states have such a law: IAPP's state privacy law chart (dated 2026-09-08,
https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/bltd4dd5b2d705252bc/blt90070182553bdfe6/State_Comp_Privacy_Law_Chart.pdf ,
read 2026-11-21) lists 23 signed laws: AL, CA, CO, CT, DE, IN, IA, KY, LA, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OK, OR, RI, TN, TX, UT,
VT, VA. The live IAPP tracker is behind a membership wall; a law signed after 2026-09-08 would not be here (UNCONFIRMED).

## Quick decision table

| State | In force | A small business is reached when | Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas, Nebraska | yes | it is NOT an SBA small business (no count); an SBA small business still needs consent before selling sensitive data | US-ST-TX, US-ST-NE |
| Minnesota | yes | 100,000 records, or 25,000 and over 25% of revenue from selling; SBA small businesses excluded except the sensitive-data sale rule | US-ST-MN |
| Connecticut | yes | 35,000 records, OR any sensitive data at all, OR it offers personal data for sale | US-ST-CT |
| Delaware | yes (lower test from 2027-01-01) | 35,000 records, or 10,000 and over 20% of revenue from selling (from 2027: 10,000, or 5,000 and 20%) | US-ST-DE |
| New Hampshire, Maryland, Rhode Island | yes | 35,000 records, or 10,000 and over 20-25% of revenue from selling | US-ST-NH, US-ST-MD, US-ST-RI |
| Montana | yes | 25,000 records, or 15,000 and over 25% of revenue from selling | US-ST-MT |
| Virginia, Colorado, Iowa, Kentucky, Indiana, Oregon, New Jersey | yes | 100,000 records, or 25,000 plus revenue from selling (the share differs) | the state's entry |
| Utah, Tennessee | yes | only with revenue of $25 million or more AND a records test | US-ST-UT, US-ST-TN |
| Oklahoma, Louisiana, Alabama, Vermont | not yet (2027-01-01, 2027-01-01, 2027-05-01, 2028-01-01) | see the entry; draft for them only if the page will still be live on that date | the state's entry |

Rhode Island also has a website rule with no size test (US-ST-RI). Florida's law reaches only $1 billion companies, except
its ban on selling sensitive data without consent (US-ST-FL).

### US-ST-VA
- **Says:** Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act, in force 2023-01-01.
- **Applies if:** business in Virginia or aimed at its residents, and 100,000 records, or 25,000 records and over 50% of
  gross revenue from selling personal data.
- **Source:** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title59.1/chapter53/section59.1-576/ ; effective date
  https://lis.virginia.gov/cgi-bin/legp604.exe?212+ful+CHAP0035 ; 45 + 45 days
  https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title59.1/chapter53/section59.1-577/ . **Read on:** 2026-11-21. **Status:** CONFIRMED (the threshold re-read by the skill's author 2026-11-21).

### US-ST-CO
- **Says:** Colorado Privacy Act, in force 2023-07-01.
- **Applies if:** business in Colorado or aimed at its residents, and 100,000 records, or any revenue or discount from
  selling personal data plus 25,000 records. No revenue floor.
- **Source:** SB21-190 as signed, https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/54661/download . **Read on:** 2026-11-21.
  **Status:** CONFIRMED (the Attorney General's page did not load).

### US-ST-CT
- **Says:** Connecticut Data Privacy Act as amended by Public Act 25-113, whose new test took effect 2026-07-01.
- **Applies if:** business in Connecticut or aimed at its residents, and in the preceding year ANY of: 35,000 records;
  controls or processes consumers' sensitive data (any amount); offers personal data for sale. A small business that
  touches sensitive data or sells data is covered.
- **Source:** https://www.cga.ct.gov/2025/ACT/PA/PDF/2025PA-00113-R00SB-01295-PA.PDF (sec. 6, "Effective July 1, 2026");
  45 + 45 days: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_743jj.htm . **Read on:** 2026-11-21. **Status:** CONFIRMED by the
  research helper; the skill's author could not re-open the PDF (certificate error on 2026-11-21).

### US-ST-UT
- **Says:** Utah Consumer Privacy Act, in force 2023-12-31 (date from the IAPP chart).
- **Applies if:** business in Utah or aimed at its residents, AND annual revenue of $25,000,000 or more, AND 100,000

- **Source:** Utah Code 13-61-102, https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter61/13-61-S102.html . **Read on:** 2026-11-21.
  **Status:** ARCHIVE (UNCONFIRMED); the answer time was not read.

### US-ST-TX
- **Says:** Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, in force 2024-07-01.
- **Applies if:** business in Texas or whose products or services Texans use, that processes or sells personal data, and
  is NOT a small business by the US SBA definition. No count or revenue test. An SBA small business may still not sell
  sensitive data without the consumer's prior consent (sec. 541.107).
- **Source:** HB 4 as enrolled, https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00004F.htm (sec. 541.002, 541.107; both re-read by the skill's author 2026-11-21).
  **Read on:** 2026-11-21. **Status:** CONFIRMED.

### US-ST-RI
- **Says:** Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (ch. 6-48.1), in force 2026-01-01. Separately, every
